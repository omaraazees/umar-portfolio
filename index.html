Custom .cv Domain

Resumedone - Product Design. Resumedone, 2026.

Mandate

Resumedone wanted a new paid add-on: a user buys their own name.cv domain and their resume goes live as a personal website on it. When I was brought in, the registrar backend was ready but there was no interface at all. I designed the whole experience inside the dashboard, from finding a name to managing the subscription, plus the website templates people publish.

Challenge

Buying a domain is a technical errand, and most job seekers have never done it. Pricing, name rules and payment were still being decided in chat while an early AI draft pointed the other way. The partner API offered one locked template, and a public site raised a real privacy question: how much contact detail should a resume show to the whole internet?

Solution

I turned the purchase into three short steps, Name, Details and Set up, with search results that show one clear price per domain per year and payment on the card already saved. The website editor reuses the resume builder's own components, so there is nothing new to learn. People pick from five templates that use their resume data exactly as written, and they decide what is public: email and phone are masked by default, with a contact form and an availability status instead.

Results

Targets set for launch: 8% of paid users adding a .cv domain in the first quarter, 70% of buyers publishing their site within a day, and 12% higher annual retention among users with a live site. Delivered as 141 dashboard screens, a mobile editor and five templates, with a screen for every failure state.

CV-Lite Help Center

CV-Lite - Product Design. CV-Lite by Resumedone, 2026.

Mandate

CV-Lite's FAQ at help.cv-lite.com was a single column of three collapsed lists: 32 links pointing to 19 articles, with no answer visible until you clicked. The product lead asked me to redesign it. I covered the home page, search, every article, and the states in between, at desktop, tablet and mobile.

Challenge

People open a help page in the middle of a problem, usually stuck on billing or an export, so every extra click costs patience. With only 19 articles, a full help center with category pages would add steps instead of saving them. The new design also had to keep the live product's colors and type, so it would feel like the same app.

Solution

I compared 15 help center homes and 19 FAQs on Mobbin, then tested six home layouts. The winner puts search first, then the most popular questions, then topics that open in place, with support always one tap away. Search suggests articles while you type and highlights the matching words. Articles read in one column with real product screenshots and end with a quick helpful or not question. Back to FAQ returns to the same topic, already open, and dead links land on a friendly 404 instead of an error.

Results

Targets set for launch: 25% fewer support tickets about the 13 most asked questions, 60% of visitors finding their answer within two clicks, and over 80% of article ratings marked helpful. Delivered as 24 boards across four widths, 32 article pages and a working coded prototype.

AI Headshot Funnel

CV-Lite - Product Design. CV-Lite by Resumedone, 2026.

Mandate

CV-Lite turns a few selfies into studio headshots, and most of its visitors arrive cold from paid ads. I was asked to redesign the whole funnel for an A/B test against the live version, from the landing page to the photo library. The old flow showed no proof of the result, left empty photo slots, and only revealed the price at the very end.

Challenge

People have to trust an unknown AI with their face, then pay before they see the full result. Ad traffic leaves fast, so every second of doubt costs a sale. On real screens the browser bar also takes around 180 px, which pushed the button that moves people forward below the fold on laptops and phones alike.

Solution

I built the funnel around one idea: show the transformation before asking for anything. The landing hero is a before and after of the same person, and every step stays short: gender, outfit, background, selfies. The paywall unlocks two finished headshots and states one honest price. Each screen is laid out at the real visible area, 1920 × 900 on desktop and 390 × 664 on mobile, so the next action is always in view.

Results

Targets set for the A/B test: 20% more visitors reaching the selfie upload, 15% higher paywall conversion, and a third fewer checkout drop-offs by showing the price up front. Built on 75 screens, 65 of them reworked so the main action never needs a scroll.

Ejemplos Curriculum

Ejemplos Curriculum - Landing Page Design. Ejemplos Curriculum, 2026.

Mandate

Ejemplos Curriculum is a Spanish-language resume site with templates, real CV examples, cover letters, and writing advice for job seekers. I designed its marketing landing page, the page most visitors see first, at desktop, tablet, and mobile widths.

Challenge

Most visitors arrive from search with one narrow question, like how to write a resignation letter, and leave once it is answered. The landing page had to show in a few seconds that the site can also help them build a CV, without the pushy habits common in this category: no "free" promise that turns into a paywall, and no social proof without data behind it.

Solution

The hero leads with what people search for, the best CV templates and writing advice, and shows real templates instead of stock art. Each section below answers one question job seekers ask, from which template to pick to how to write a cover letter, and ends with a clear way into the builder. The AI tools sit together in one dark band so they read as a feature, not a pop-up. The header keeps templates, examples, letters, and sign-in one click away, so the page works as a hub for the rest of the site.

Results

Targets set for launch: 30% more visitors reaching a template from the landing page, 20% more visitors scrolling past the hero, and a higher share of search visitors starting a CV. Delivered as one landing page at four widths, ready for development.
